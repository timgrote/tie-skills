# Cold Email Format

Standard structure for ALL cold outreach emails. This is the format Tim wants every future cold email to follow, no matter who writes it. Keep it tight — cold prospects skim; every word must earn its place.

## Structure

1. **Subject line** — show the value or the pain up front. One line that makes the reader think "this is about me / my problem." No clickbait, no generic greeting.

2. **Three very short paragraphs** (2–3 sentences each, ideally fewer):
   - **Who you are** — one line of credibility and relevance to *them*. Why should they listen? Connect it to their world, not yours.
   - **The vision** — the value. What's in it for them? Paint the outcome clearly.
   - **The homework** — a specific, low-effort, concrete thing for them to do or check. Make it trivially easy to say yes to.

3. **Clear CTA** — one direct, singular ask. Do not bury it or offer a menu of options.

## Rules

- Three short paragraphs is the *ceiling*, not a target. Shorter is better.
- One idea per paragraph.
- The homework and CTA should feel nearly effortless for the reader.
- Write plainly. No hype, no feature-dump, no claims you can't back up (per Tim's preference for honesty over over-promising).
- No attachment wall, no multi-link clutter in the first touch.

## Example skeleton

> **Subject:** Cutting estimate time on irrigation jobs
>
> (Who you are) I build irrigation design software used by landscape contractors.
>
> (Vision) Contractors using it cut quoting time from hours to minutes and stop under-pricing jobs.
>
> (Homework) Reply with a sample plan and I'll show you what it would produce for it.
>
> **CTA:** Want to see it on one of your real drawings?

## Verification

Before sending, check:
- [ ] Subject shows value or pain
- [ ] Exactly three very short paragraphs (or fewer)
- [ ] Each paragraph does one job (who / vision / homework)
- [ ] One clear CTA
- [ ] No over-promises or unverifiable claims
